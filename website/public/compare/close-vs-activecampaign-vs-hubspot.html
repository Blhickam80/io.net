Close vs ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot: Best CRM for a Service Business

Most local and home service businesses don't need everything a modern CRM can do — they need to stop losing track of leads and follow-ups. Here's how these three commonly recommended options actually differ for that specific job.

Quick answer

Close is built around calling and a simple sales pipeline — good fit if your business closes deals mostly over the phone (contractors, real estate, agencies) and you want something fast to set up without a lot of marketing-automation overhead. ActiveCampaign combines a CRM with genuinely strong email/SMS automation — a better fit if nurturing leads over time (follow-ups, reminders, review requests) matters as much as tracking the pipeline itself. HubSpot is the broadest platform with a real free tier, but most of what makes it powerful lives behind paid tiers that get expensive fast, and the learning curve is steeper than the other two for a small team.

Who each is actually for

Close fits a business where the CRM's main job is "don't let a phone lead go cold" — roofers, contractors, real estate teams, and agencies that close deals through conversations rather than long email sequences.

ActiveCampaign fits a business that wants automated follow-up sequences doing real work in the background — appointment reminders, review requests, re-engagement campaigns — on top of basic pipeline tracking.

HubSpot fits a business planning to grow into a bigger marketing/sales operation and wants one platform to grow into, and is willing to accept a steeper learning curve and rising costs in exchange for that breadth.

Our take

Start with what's actually breaking down in your business today. If leads are going cold because nobody's calling them back fast enough, Close solves that directly with the least setup. If you're losing customers to poor follow-up over weeks and months, ActiveCampaign's automation does more of that work for you. HubSpot is worth the extra complexity mainly if you're already planning to build out a bigger marketing function, not just fix today's lead-tracking problem.
