## Winners (what worked)
- **Getting to live was the single biggest unlock of the week.** Diagnosing the failed first deploy precisely (via workflow job logs, not guesswork) and fixing it in one round-trip with the operator kept the launch on the "this weekend" timeline the operator asked for.
- **Verifying Votel.ai's real terms before shipping anything** turned a previously-unverifiable, deprioritized program into the #1 live revenue asset, without compromising the editorial recommendation on the GoHighLevel-vs-Votel page (handled via an explicit transparency note rather than silently steering readers toward the higher-commission option).
- **The Customer Monetization Stack rule held across all 11 published pages** — every money page cross-sells the full stack (receptionist + CRM + scheduling + reputation), not a single offer. This was a direct correction from the operator earlier in the week and it's now structurally enforced in `CONTENT_PLAN.md`.

## Learnings
- The video-content misjudgment (logged 2026-08-27) and the resulting strategy correction — video is on-demand, not a cadence — should be treated as the template for catching drift going forward: something looked like progress (a produced asset) without being progress (distributable content), and got caught because the operator pushed back rather than because a review caught it. This weekly review process exists specifically to catch the next one of these before the operator has to.
- Standing "go for it" authorization on merge/deploy mechanics worked cleanly across 8 PR ship cycles this week with zero errors after the initial Pages-config issue — the risk in autonomous shipping this week was never the git mechanics, it was making sure editorial integrity (Votel transparency note) didn't get compromised by a monetization incentive. That's the pattern to keep watching, not the mechanics.

GoHighLevel and the rest of the shortlist joining (ACCESS_NEEDED #5) should not be treated as low-urgency just because Votel unblocked things.

Not a problem yet (content is genuinely useful and correctly structured either way), but it argues against building more page types on speculation before the SEO/keyword expansion has a chance to surface what's actually working.
